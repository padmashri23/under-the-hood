GPT-6.2 vs Gemini 4 Carbon vs Claude Fable 5.2: which one should a normal business use?

Three top AI models became widely available in November 2026, eight days apart. Here's what each one is good at, what it really costs, and why most small businesses should start one step down.

Researched & written November 2026 · ~9 min read · Sources cited throughout

Key points
  • All three companies say most everyday work should go to a cheaper model: GPT-6.2 Sol, Gemini 4 Argon or Claude Opus 5.5.
  • The same price tag doesn't mean the same bill. In one independent test, Fable 5.2 cost 6.6 times as much per task as GPT-6.2 Astra.
  • For office work, two cheaper Claude models scored higher than all three top models.
  • For multi-step work across business apps, Gemini 4 Carbon did best of the three.
  • From December 1, 2026, Gemini 4 Carbon costs twice its launch price.
  • Check your plan before you pay. Not every team plan includes the newest model.

🤔 The short answer

You run a small business. You keep hearing about three new AI models. Which one should you pay for?

The honest answer: probably none of them. At least, not first.

In plain words: Each AI company sells a range of models. Think of a car maker that sells a family car and a sports car. The three models in this article are the sports cars. They're powerful, but most daily trips don't need one.

The companies say so themselves:

  • OpenAI calls GPT-6.2 Sol, not Astra, "our go-to model for everyday work".
  • Google says "Argon remains the ideal choice for the majority of software engineering and knowledge work". It keeps Carbon for the hardest tasks.
  • Anthropic writes: "For most coding and knowledge tasks, we recommend that users start with Claude Opus 5.5, our strongest daily driver".

Good to know: In this article, a "flagship" is a company's most powerful model. An "everyday model" is the cheaper one each company suggests for normal work.

📖 Words you'll see in this article

🧾 Meet the three

In plain words: Three companies made their best models widely available in November 2026, within eight days of each other.

GPT-6.2 Astra (OpenAI)

  • Released: November 17, 2026.
  • What it's for: OpenAI calls it "our new frontier model for your hardest projects". A frontier model is one built to push the limits of what AI can do.
  • API price: $8 per million input tokens and $40 per million output tokens.
  • Everyday sibling: GPT-6.2 Sol, released the same day. It costs $2 and $10.

Gemini 4 Carbon (Google)

  • Announced: October 28, 2026, for a small group of security experts. Open to all paying developers and businesses on November 10.
  • What it's for: Google calls it "our strongest model".
  • API price: $2 and $10 through November 30, 2026. $4 and $20 from December 1.
  • Context window: about a million tokens, enough for several long books.
  • Where to get it: the API, Gemini for business, or the Gemini app, rolling out to Google AI Ultra subscribers.

Claude Fable 5.2 (Anthropic)

  • Released: November 9, 2026.
  • What it's for: "For demanding reasoning and long-horizon agentic work". That means long, hard jobs the model works through on its own, step by step.
  • API price: $8 and $40, the same as Astra.
  • Context window: up to a million tokens.
  • Everyday sibling: Claude Opus 5.5. It costs $4 and $20.

Good to know: "Input" is the text you send to the model. "Output" is the text it writes back. For all three flagships, output costs five times as much as input.

📊 How they actually score

In plain words: Every company chooses which tests to show in its own launch post. So we used independent leaderboards instead. There, one outside tester runs every model the same way.

Overall smarts

Artificial Analysis is an independent testing company. It combines many tests into one score, called the Intelligence Index. Higher is better. Think of it like a school report card that averages many different subjects.

  • The flagships: Fable 5.2 scored 65. Astra scored 62. Carbon scored 60.
  • The everyday models: Opus 5.5 scored 59. Sol scored 57. Argon scored 51.

Now look at Sol. It scored five points below Astra, at about a quarter of the cost per task.

Good to know: The cost scale in Figure 2 isn't even: each step is double or more.

Office work

AA-Briefcase tests "realistic business workflows that require deliverables such as spreadsheets, presentations, and memos." Then it rates the results with an Elo score.

  • The flagships: Fable 5.2 (1783) beat Astra (1642) and Carbon (1611) by a wide margin.
  • The surprise: two cheaper Claude models beat Fable. Sonnet 5.5 ($2 and $10) scored 1812. Opus 5.5 ($4 and $20) scored 1797.
  • A tie: GPT-6.2 Sol (1612) and Carbon (1611) finished level.

Good to know: Elo is a rating system first made for chess players. A gap of a few points means very little. Each score has a margin of error of about 8 to 12 points.

Workflows and chat

A workflow is a job with several steps, often across different apps. This is where Gemini 4 Carbon shines.

  • AutomationBench: built by Zapier, a company that links business apps. It tests multi-step jobs across business software. In Artificial Analysis's run, Carbon scored 77.8%. Only Google's own Gemini 4 Neon scored higher.
  • Anthropic's own table: Carbon 53.9%, Fable 51.4%.
  • Arena: on this leaderboard, people compare two answers and vote for the better one. On November 22, Carbon was first on its text leaderboard. Fable 5.2 was eighth.

💸 What it really costs

In plain words: The price on the label isn't what you'll pay. Your bill depends on how many tokens the model uses to finish a job.

Same price tag, different bill

  • Astra and Fable both list at $8 and $40.
  • In Artificial Analysis's tests, one Astra task cost $4.00.
  • One Fable task cost $26.54.
  • That's 6.6 times as much, for the same price tag.

Why? These models "think" before they answer, and that thinking is written in tokens you pay for. Google's price list says output costs "including thinking tokens". Both models ran on the Max setting. Fable used far more tokens.

Good to know: More on token bills in The True Economics of Inference.

Gemini 4 Carbon has two prices

  • Launch price, through November 30, 2026: $2 per million input tokens, $10 per million output tokens.
  • Standard price, from December 1, 2026: $4 and $20.

Plan your budget on the standard price.

How long you wait

Artificial Analysis also timed each task. Sol and Astra finished fastest. Carbon and Fable took about 40 minutes, roughly 2.5 times as long as Astra.

Good to know: These were long test tasks. Compare the order, not the exact minutes.

Seats or API?

A team that uses a chat app pays per seat. You only need the API to build AI into your own app or website.

Worked example, a team of 10: ChatGPT Business and Claude Team charge the same list prices.

  • 10 standard seats: $200 a month billed yearly ($2,400 a year), or $250 billed monthly.
  • 10 premium seats: $1,000 a month billed yearly, or $1,250 billed monthly.
  • Team size: both need at least 2 people. ChatGPT Business goes up to 200, Claude Team up to 150.

Good to know: Claude's pricing page lists Fable 5.2 on premium seats, so budget for those if you need Fable. Google Workspace prices vary by country.

🏢 Does your plan include the flagship?

In plain words: A team plan doesn't always unlock the newest model. Check before you pay.

🧭 So which one should you pick?

In plain words: Pick by the job, not the brand. Here's a starting point built from the data above.

Good to know: If you can only buy one thing, buy a team plan from the company whose tools you already use. Use the everyday model most of the day. Switch to the flagship for the hard stuff.

Where to start

  1. List five real tasks you'd hand to AI this month.
  2. Try the everyday model first. Use Sol, Opus 5.5 or Argon.
  3. Try the flagship on the same five. Keep it only where the answers are clearly better.
  4. Check your plan includes the model you picked.
  5. Watch the real cost. On the API, track cost per task, not list price.

❓ Quick answers

Can I use GPT-6.2 on ChatGPT Business?

Business isn't named in OpenAI's launch post, and the Business pricing page still lists older models, like GPT-6.1 Sol and GPT-6 Astra. Check your workspace's model menu.

Will they train on my business data?

"Training" means using your messages to improve future models. All three say they don't do this on their business plans by default. The exact quotes are in the plan table above.

Which flagship is cheapest?

Per token, Gemini 4 Carbon: even at its standard price, it's half the price of Astra or Fable. Per task, Astra was cheapest in Artificial Analysis's tests.

✅ Conclusion

All three flagships are strong. Fable 5.2 scores highest overall. Astra comes close for far less per task. Carbon leads on work across business apps.

But most business days are emails, documents and spreadsheets, and cheaper models like Opus 5.5 and Sol handle those well. Start there. Pay for the top shelf only when a real task proves you need it.
